# Fee Abstraction Models — Research Findings

Design exploration for a cross-chain gas / fee abstraction product. Captured during study of Paima Engine and adjacent patterns. Companion to [PAIMA_DUAL_CHAIN_PAIRING.md](./PAIMA_DUAL_CHAIN_PAIRING.md).

Two product targets in scope:

- **Kuira-internal**: gas abstraction inside the Kuira product so users do not face native gas tokens
- **Standalone middleware**: a reusable infra product that other dApps can integrate

---

## The core asymmetry

The reason cross-chain fee abstraction is harder for Midnight than for EVM or Solana is that DUST is not the same *kind of thing* as SOL or ETH.

| Property | DUST | SOL / ETH |
|---|---|---|
| How it exists | Generated by holding NIGHT | Bought on a market |
| Transferable | No | Yes |
| Has a market price | No (not directly) | Yes |
| Decays | Yes (capacity model) | No (balance model) |
| Conceptual category | Capacity allocation | Unit of value |

DUST is closer to **AWS free-tier credits** than to a currency. You cannot take your AWS credits and pay Azure with them. They only buy capacity on the system that issued them. SOL and ETH are *fungible value* — they buy capacity, but they are also worth something independent of any single chain.

This asymmetry does not yield to cleverness. There is no direct conservation law connecting non-transferable, non-priced, regenerating capacity to transferable, priced, accumulating value. Anything that looks like "DUST pays for ETH gas" is actually doing one of the four things in the next section underneath.

## The four honest models

### Model 1 — Operator subsidy (DUST as proof-of-standing)

The user does not actually fund cross-chain gas. The operator does. The user's DUST (or NIGHT holdings) is interpreted as a **Sybil-resistance signal**: "this is a real Kuira user, not a bot, so we are willing to spend marketing budget on their gas."

- Economically: customer acquisition cost, not a value swap
- Best fit when volume × per-tx cost is small relative to retention value
- No working capital risk beyond budget allocation
- Boring, easy, immediately shippable

### Model 2 — NIGHT-burns-for-gas

If the user wants real value to flow across chains, the asset that crosses must be **NIGHT**, not DUST. NIGHT is transferable and has a market price. User burns or sells NIGHT, operator credits a gas budget denominated in whatever the user is targeting.

- Real value exchange, conservation respected
- Consumes the user's *capital*, not their *capacity*
- UX feels like spending savings, not "free chain usage"
- Requires operator to maintain a NIGHT trading position

### Model 3 — Staked-NIGHT-as-collateral

User locks NIGHT in an operator-run vault. Two value streams result:

- The lock's **DUST generation** flows naturally to fund the user's Midnight ops (matches the substrate's intent — NIGHT holders get Midnight capacity)
- The **locked NIGHT itself** serves as collateral against cross-chain gas the operator advances in ETH/SOL on the user's behalf

Withdrawal returns the NIGHT minus any cross-chain advances, or is denied until repayment.

- Single deposit, multi-chain coverage
- Respects DUST's capacity nature on Midnight
- Uses NIGHT's value nature as the working-capital backing for cross-chain ops
- Operator float risk is bounded by collateral, not unbacked
- Structurally novel — only possible because of Midnight's two-tier capacity/value token design
- Hard parts: NIGHT price volatility, liquidation rules, withdrawal latency, possible regulatory framing as a lending product

### Model 4 — Don't unify (per-chain native paths)

Each chain pays for itself in its own currency:

- Midnight ops: user's own DUST (free if they hold NIGHT)
- Solana ops: Octane-style USDC relayer
- EVM ops: ERC-4337 paymaster paid in USDC

The "engine" is API + routing + accounting only. Value never crosses chains.

- Cleanest economics, no cross-chain risk
- Worst UX — user may need multiple funding sources
- Cheapest to ship
- Commoditized — competes directly with existing paymasters

## Volume-grounded sizing

Rough order-of-magnitude for the initial use case (Midnight Kicks-shaped activity):

- ~5 tx per match
- ~500 matches per day
- ~2,500 tx/day total

At baseline Solana fees (~$0.0001/tx), that's ~$0.25/day in raw gas. At low Arbitrum fees, similar order. This is rounding error against any plausible retention-value justification.

**Implication**: for Kuira-internal at launch volumes, the question is not "how do we recoup gas" but "is the cost worth tracking at all." Model 1 (subsidize) is correct until volume crosses thresholds where it matters. Designing anything more elaborate for internal use right now is over-engineering.

## How the existing toolchain maps

| Chain | Native fee abstraction primitive |
|---|---|
| Midnight | NIGHT holdings → DUST generation. Already abstract from the user's perspective if they hold NIGHT |
| EVM | ERC-4337 paymasters (Pimlico, Biconomy, ZeroDev, Alchemy) — token-denominated gas via off-chain quoting + on-chain paymaster contracts |
| Solana | Fee payer separation + Octane-style co-signing relayers — user signs a tx including a USDC transfer, relayer co-signs as fee_payer |

Models 1, 2, and 4 can all be built on top of these existing primitives. Model 3 requires custom collateral logic in a NIGHT-aware vault — there is no off-the-shelf equivalent because no other ecosystem has the capacity/value two-tier structure.

## Recommendation framing

The two product targets pull toward different answers.

**For Kuira-internal**: ship Model 1 at launch. Volume is too small for anything else to pay for its own complexity. Revisit when daily tx volume crosses an order of magnitude where gas spend becomes a meaningful budget line.

**For standalone middleware**: this is the real strategic choice.

- **Safe path**: Model 4 — aggregator middleware on top of existing paymasters. Ships fastest, lowest risk, also lowest moat. Competes with Pimlico/Biconomy/Octane on UX and pricing.
- **Bet path**: Model 3 — NIGHT-collateral-backed multi-chain gas. Only viable if NIGHT-as-collateral is acceptable to enough users, but if it works, it is structurally uncopyable without inventing an equivalent of NIGHT/DUST first.

The bet path is the only one that exploits Midnight's unique token structure rather than treating it as a constraint to work around.

## Things that need answering before committing

- Is the standalone middleware meant to compete in the broader paymaster market, or specifically to serve Midnight-adjacent products?
- How sensitive is the target user to NIGHT price volatility while their gas is collateralized?
- What does the regulatory framing look like for Model 3 in target jurisdictions (locked collateral funding payments on behalf of users sounds like custody and/or credit issuance depending on framing)?
- Does interoperability scope (currently deferred) eventually require Model 3, or can Model 4 satisfy it indefinitely?
- Where does this sit relative to [[project_ows_alignment]] — does any of this conflict with Open Wallet Standards alignment?

## Open questions deferred

- Solana-specific deep dive on Octane and priority-fee handling during congestion
- Concrete liquidation rules and oracle design for Model 3
- Cost modeling at projected scale ranges (10k, 100k, 1M tx/day)
- Whether a hybrid (Model 1 for low-touch users, Model 3 for power users) is worth the bifurcated UX
